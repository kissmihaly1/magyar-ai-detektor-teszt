Which AI detector works best for Hungarian?

We tested seven well-known AI detectors on the same 1,000 Hungarian texts. Preds 3, a detector built specifically for Hungarian, came first with 99.6% accuracy and did not flag a single one of the 500 human-written texts as AI.

Key findings

What the numbers mean

Percentages hide real people. These are the raw counts behind them, out of 500 human-written and 500 AI-written texts.

01

Zero false accusations

Preds 3 flagged none of the 500 human-written texts as AI. ZeroGPT flagged 174 and Originality.ai flagged 163, roughly one in three.

02

Almost nothing slips through

Preds 3 missed only 4 of the 500 AI texts. Smodin missed 369, isgen.ai 338 and Plag.hu 233, even though they rarely flagged humans.

03

Hungarian training data matters

ZeroGPT, isgen.ai and Smodin score an AUROC between 0.69 and 0.77, so their scores barely separate human from AI text. No threshold can fix that, and it usually points to little Hungarian training data. Preds 3, built for Hungarian, scores 0.9996.

Method

Transparent and repeatable

The 1,000 texts come from 9 domains, with the same number of human and AI texts in each, so the topic cannot give the label away. Lengths are matched within each domain, from 80 to 995 words, with a median of 198.

The AI texts were generated fresh for this test and were never used to train Preds, so our own model had no head start.

FAQ

Common questions

What is the best AI detector for Hungarian?

On the open HuDetect-1k benchmark, Preds 3 led every metric: 99.6% accuracy, a 0% false positive rate and 0.9996 AUROC. GPTZero came second with 90.1% accuracy.

Do English-first AI detectors work on Hungarian text?

Results vary widely. In the benchmark, ZeroGPT and Originality.ai flagged roughly one in three human-written Hungarian texts as AI, while Smodin and isgen.ai missed most AI-written texts.

Who ran the benchmark?

The dataset and the benchmark were created by the developer of Preds. Because Preds is one of the detectors compared, every text, label and per-detector result is public, so anyone can check and repeat the test.

Can I reproduce the results?

Yes. The 1,000 texts and labels are public on Hugging Face under CC BY 4.0, and the per-detector confusion counts are available as CSV and JSON.

Check the results yourself

The 1,000 texts, the labels and the per-detector results are public. Download the dataset, run any detector on it and compare the numbers.
